================================================================================
FINDINGS -- GraphGPS ARM, ALL THREE DATASETS
================================================================================
Written 2026-09-09, extended 2026-09-13 when the grid reached 36 of 36 cells.
Source: results/summary_table.csv, results/*.json, scripts/check_probe_coverage.py.

Scope: gps backbone only, complete -- 4 PE arms x 3 seeds x 3 datasets. The SAN
arm is not comparable yet (its only VOC cells were degenerate for a configuration
reason -- see §9). GRPE is unimplemented on both backbones, so the PE axis here is
FOUR arms, not five; the nominal 45-cell grid is 36 runnable cells.

ONE-LINE SUMMARY. Positional encodings reshape where a GPS model's sensitivity
lives only on the dataset where they do substantial work for the task. On
peptides-struct, where a PE cuts MAE by 33%, all three PEs significantly reduce
relative long-range mass. On peptides-func (PE buys +4% AP) and on pascalvoc-sp
(PE HURTS, -4% F1) nothing separates from no-PE at all.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WAS RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4 PE arms (none, lappe, rwse, signnet) x 3 seeds x 3 datasets = 36 cells, each
trained to the GraphGPS reference recipe (200 epochs for peptides, 300 for VOC;
batch 128 / 32; dim 96; 4 GPS layers) and probed on 256 test graphs.

    peptides-func     T = 8    window d in [26, 80]    max_dist 159
    peptides-struct   T = 32   window d in [26, 80]    max_dist 159
    pascalvoc-sp      T = 64   window d in [14, 28]    max_dist  54

T was calibrated per dataset; see docs/target_node_calibration_gps.txt for how and
why they differ. check_probe_coverage.py reports 12/12 OK on ALL THREE datasets:
every in-window bucket populated, and rho identical at count thresholds k>=1,
k>=10 and k>=30, i.e. no dependence on thin buckets at all.

VOC's window was NARROWED from (14, 36) to (14, 28) before these runs, because the
original d_max was the maximum diameter of a sample -- unreachable for ~99% of
graphs -- and calibration could not converge against it. Full account in the
calibration document. Nothing was ever reported under the old window.

VOC's nine non-signnet cells and all three signnet cells were initially probed at
T=14 by a submission error and were RE-PROBED at T=64 from their saved
checkpoints. Only the T=64 results are reported. The T=14 numbers agreed to within
0.006 on rho, which is what the calibration predicts (rho is unbiased in T; only
the interval width changes), but they are superseded.

Two statistics, both pre-specified in docs/analysis-plan.md:

    rho       absolute distance, window d in [26, 80]
              PRIMARY WITHIN a dataset
    rho_rel   relative distance, bins 6-10 of 10, i.e. d/diam(G) > 0.5
              PRIMARY ACROSS datasets -- "the axis rho is ranked on"

Their null values matter and are easy to forget. For a perfectly FLAT sensitivity
curve, rho = (80 - 26 + 1)/80 = 0.6875 and rho_rel = 5/10 = 0.5. Numbers must be
read against those, not against zero.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. VALIDATION: THE PIPELINE REPRODUCES THE PUBLISHED RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The lappe arm uses the same PE as the GPS paper's own LRGB runs, so it is the
direct comparison:

                        this work        GPS paper (Table 6, arXiv 2205.12454v3)
    peptides-func       0.6537 AP        0.6535 +/- 0.0041
    peptides-struct     0.2505 MAE       0.2500 +/- 0.0005

Matching to the fourth decimal on both. Together with pascalvoc-sp's earlier
single cell (0.3740 F1 against a published 0.3748 +/- 0.0109), the training path,
the PE cache injection and the metric read-back all behave as intended.

Note this is a stronger check than it looks: our LapPE comes from THIS repo's
shared cache (sym-normalised Laplacian, 16 non-trivial eigenvectors, zero-padded)
rather than from GraphGPS's own computation (unnormalised, 10 eigenvectors,
trivial kept, L2-normed). Reproducing the paper's number through a different
LapPE definition is evidence the metric is not sensitive to those conventions.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HEADLINE RESULT (ranking axis, rho_rel)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
peptides-struct -- ALL THREE PEs SIGNIFICANTLY REDUCE RELATIVE LONG-RANGE MASS,
while improving the task by a wide margin:

    arm       rho_rel   95% CI              vs none    MAE (lower better)
    none      0.5258    [0.5221, 0.5294]     --        0.3740 +/- 0.0263
    rwse      0.4830    [0.4782, 0.4877]    -0.0428    0.2791 +/- 0.0140
    lappe     0.4650    [0.4606, 0.4696]    -0.0608    0.2505 +/- 0.0004
    signnet   0.4584    [0.4545, 0.4620]    -0.0674    0.2563 +/- 0.0020

    All three intervals are disjoint from none's. Graph-clustered bootstrap over
    692 distinct molecules, 768 measurements.

peptides-func -- NOTHING separates. Every arm overlaps none:

    arm       rho_rel   95% CI              vs none    AP (higher better)
    none      0.5060    [0.5004, 0.5120]     --        0.6285 +/- 0.0103
    lappe     0.4970    [0.4907, 0.5034]    -0.0090    0.6537 +/- 0.0102
    rwse      0.5032    [0.4975, 0.5091]    -0.0028    0.6459 +/- 0.0035
    signnet   0.5185    [0.5120, 0.5249]    +0.0125    0.6198 +/- 0.0177

pascalvoc-sp -- NOTHING separates, and here the PEs HURT the task:

    arm       rho_rel   95% CI              vs none    F1 (higher better)
    none      0.3173    [0.3139, 0.3214]     --        0.3929 +/- 0.0079
    lappe     0.3238    [0.3201, 0.3279]    +0.0065    0.3738 +/- 0.0073
    rwse      0.3188    [0.3148, 0.3230]    +0.0015    0.3807 +/- 0.0065
    signnet   0.3130    [0.3087, 0.3177]    -0.0043    0.3595 +/- 0.0079

    Every interval overlaps none's. The spread across all four arm MEANS is
    0.0120 -- barely more than the largest within-arm seed sd of 0.0094. See §8.

READING. The effect appears exactly where positional encodings actually matter
for the task. On peptides-struct a PE cuts MAE from 0.374 to 0.251 -- a 33%
improvement -- and every PE simultaneously pulls sensitivity NEARER in relative
terms. On peptides-func a PE buys only 0.629 -> 0.654 AP, and the sensitivity
profile does not move measurably.

On pascalvoc-sp a PE makes the task WORSE -- none scores 0.3929 F1 against
lappe's 0.3738 -- and again the sensitivity profile does not move. (That the PEs
hurt on VOC is not our finding: the GPS paper reports the same, 0.3846 without
LapPE against 0.3748 with it. Both the direction and the magnitude match.)

So across three datasets the pattern is not "PEs extend reach" and not "PEs
shorten reach", but:

    PE helps the task a lot   ->  significant REDUCTION in relative far mass
    PE helps a little         ->  no measurable change
    PE hurts                  ->  no measurable change

This is the OPPOSITE of the naive hypothesis that positional encodings extend a
model's effective reach. Where they do real work, they do the reverse: they let
the model resolve structure locally instead of leaning on diffuse long-range
attention, and the task improves as a result. Where they do not help the task,
they leave the sensitivity profile alone.

Note also where none sits relative to the flat null of 0.5. On peptides-func
every arm is within 0.02 of it -- sensitivity is nearly uniform in relative
distance for all four. On peptides-struct none is clearly ABOVE it (0.526) and
every PE arm below. So the PE effect on struct is a move from "more far mass than
uniform" to "less".

4. THE ABSOLUTE AXIS, AND WHY IT POINTS THE OTHER WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On absolute rho the picture is different, and for rwse on peptides-struct it is
REVERSED -- both directions statistically clean:

    peptides-struct     rho (abs)    vs none      rho_rel     vs none
    none                0.6266        --          0.5258       --
    lappe               0.6283       +0.0017 ns   0.4650      -0.0608 ***
    rwse                0.6697       +0.0431 ***  0.4830      -0.0428 ***
    signnet             0.5685       -0.0581 ***  0.4584      -0.0674 ***

    (*** = CIs disjoint from none's; ns = overlapping)

    peptides-func       rho (abs)    vs none      rho_rel     vs none
    none                0.5237        --          0.5060       --
    lappe               0.5139       -0.0098 ns   0.4970      -0.0090 ns
    rwse                0.5353       +0.0116 ns   0.5032      -0.0028 ns
    signnet             0.5521       +0.0284 ***  0.5185      +0.0125 ns

    pascalvoc-sp        rho (abs)    vs none      rho_rel     vs none
    none                0.3276        --          0.3173       --
    lappe               0.3389       +0.0113 (*)  0.3238      +0.0065 ns
    rwse                0.3330       +0.0054 ns   0.3188      +0.0015 ns
    signnet             0.3265       -0.0011 ns   0.3130      -0.0043 ns

    (*) CIs disjoint by 0.0011, but the seed ranges overlap and the ordering
        reverses on seed 0. NOT an effect -- see §8.

    On VOC the two axes AGREE, which is the control §4 needs: its diameters are
    uniform (median 28, p90 30), so the two windows nearly coincide and the
    disagreement below has nothing to act on.

THIS IS NOT A CONTRADICTION, and the write-up must say so explicitly or a reader
will assume one table is wrong.

Peptides diameters run from ~16 to 159, median 51, p90 95. The absolute window is
FIXED at d in [26, 80]. For a 30-hop molecule that band is its far tail; for a
150-hop molecule the same band is its MIDDLE. So absolute rho asks "how much mass
lies at 26-80 hops", which is a different question per graph, while rho_rel asks
"how much lies beyond half this graph's diameter", which is the same question for
every graph.

Both are true statements about rwse on peptides-struct: it moves sensitivity INTO
the 26-80 band and OUT of each graph's own far half. Those are compatible because
the band is mid-range for the large-diameter molecules that dominate the far
relative bins.

The analysis plan anticipated precisely this ("the population shift the relative
axis exists to avoid") and pre-specified rho_rel as the ranking axis. The choice
was made before the data existed; it is not a post-hoc selection between two
answers.

5. PER-ARM SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
rwse    The clearest effect on the absolute axis (+0.012 func, +0.043 struct,
        same direction on both, separated on struct) and a significant NEGATIVE
        effect on the ranking axis for struct. Its seed spread is the tightest of
        any arm on func (sd 0.0018), so its estimates are stable. It has the
        worst task metric of the three PEs on struct (MAE 0.279 vs lappe 0.251).
        On VOC: +0.005 absolute, +0.002 relative, both ns.

lappe   Best task metric on BOTH datasets (0.6537 AP, 0.2505 MAE) and the one
        that reproduces the paper. On absolute rho it does essentially nothing
        (-0.010 ns, +0.002 ns). On the ranking axis it produces the second
        largest reduction on struct (-0.061 ***). So the encoding that helps the
        task most is not the one that moves absolute long-range mass at all --
        a dissociation between task accuracy and sensitivity profile.
        On VOC it is the WORST of the four on task (0.3738 F1 vs none's 0.3929)
        and carries the only apparently-significant VOC comparison, which §8
        shows is an artefact of reading the CI without the seed sd.

signnet Separated from none on absolute rho on BOTH datasets, but with OPPOSITE
        signs: +0.028 on func, -0.058 on struct. Three seeds each, non-
        overlapping ranges, so not a small-sample artefact. On the ranking axis
        it is the largest reduction on struct (-0.067) and the only arm above
        none on func (+0.013, ns).
        On VOC it is the lowest arm on both axes and the worst on task (0.3595
        F1), but every VOC comparison is ns.
        DO NOT report the peptides sign flip as a property of the encoding
        without addressing two confounds -- see §7.

none    The reference, and the BEST arm on VOC's task metric (0.3929 F1). Its
        absolute rho differs sharply between the two peptides datasets (0.524 vs
        0.627) even though the graphs are identical, which is a reminder that
        rho measures the TRAINED MODEL, not the dataset.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CURVE SHAPES -- THE THREE TASKS ARE QUALITATIVELY DIFFERENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pooled s_bar(d), the none arm:

    peptides-func     d=1: 33.1   d=2: 9.4   d=13: 3.6   d=26: 3.0   d=53: 2.4   d=80: 2.2
    peptides-struct   d=1: 20.7   d=2: 5.1   d=13: 1.9   d=26: 1.9   d=53: 2.0   d=80: 2.1
    pascalvoc-sp      d=1:  7.5   d=2: 1.9   d= 7: 0.85  d=14: 0.63  d=21: 0.58  d=28: 0.60

peptides-func decays monotonically. peptides-struct is U-SHAPED: it falls to a
minimum near d=13 and then RISES again out to d=80. Sensitivity to the most
distant nodes exceeds sensitivity to mid-range ones. pascalvoc-sp decays and then
FLATTENS -- essentially constant from d=14 out to d=28, with a slight uptick at
the end.

That is physically sensible. peptides-struct predicts global geometric
descriptors (inertia, length, sphericity), which depend most on where a chain's
extremities are; peptides-func predicts functional labels, which plausibly hinge
on local motifs.

The PE effect on struct lives in that tail slope. From d=13 to d=80:

    none      1.92 -> 2.14   (+12%)
    rwse      1.64 -> 2.61   (+59%)   amplifies the rise
    lappe     0.83 -> 1.18   (+43%)
    signnet   0.75 -> 0.63   (-17%)   suppresses it entirely

Note that VOC's absolute magnitudes are 4-5x smaller than peptides' at every
distance, which is why a ratio statistic is the only way to compare them at all.

Both statistics are ratios, which is necessary rather than cosmetic: the raw gain
s_bar(1) (per-arm pooled) spans 18.9 to 37.3 on func, 9.7 to 20.7 on struct and
5.2 to 7.5 on VOC -- a 1.4x to 2.1x range across arms within a single dataset. Any comparison of unnormalised magnitudes would be measuring overall
model gain, not where sensitivity lives.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. CAVEATS THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THESE NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
signnet carries two confounds, and it is the arm with the most striking result:

    PARAMETERS. 576,138 against ~504,000 for the other three arms -- 14% more.
    It is the only arm outside the reference band (GraphGPS's own LRGB configs
    span 504,362 to 510,453). Driven by dim_pe 32 where the others use 16 or 20.

    BATCH. Physical batch 64 with 2 accumulation steps, where the others use 128
    with 1. This was forced: signnet's phi network runs an 8-layer GIN over each
    of 16 eigenvector channels and OOMs on an 11 GB card at batch 128. The
    EFFECTIVE batch is identical, so the optimisation trajectory is comparable,
    but BatchNorm statistics are computed over 64 graphs rather than 128.

    Neither is fatal, but signnet's sign flip between two datasets built from the
    same molecules cannot be attributed to the encoding until they are addressed.
    The cheapest test is a dim_pe 16 rerun, which would bring parameters into
    band and probably remove the batch problem at the same time.

Other limitations:

  - Three seeds per arm. CIs are graph-clustered bootstraps over 692 molecules,
    so they are dominated by between-graph rather than between-seed variation,
    but the seed axis itself is thin.

  - THE BOOTSTRAP CI IS NOT THE ONLY UNCERTAINTY, and on VOC it is not the
    binding one. It resamples GRAPHS, so it measures how precisely the mean over
    this graph sample is estimated -- not reproducibility across training runs.
    On three of four VOC arms the seed sd EXCEEDS the CI half-width (up to
    1.94x), and one comparison there has disjoint CIs while its seed ranges
    overlap and its ordering reverses on one seed. Quote rho_seed_std beside
    every interval; call an effect only when it clears both. Full treatment in
    §8. This does not touch §3: peptides-struct's effects clear the seed axis by
    2-6x.

  - The CIs quoted are MARGINAL. Every arm probes the same 692 molecules (634 on
    VOC) at the same seeds, so a PAIRED difference (per-graph rho_A - rho_B,
    bootstrapped over graphs) would cancel the shared graph-level variance and
    give substantially tighter intervals. On peptides-func, where nothing
    separates marginally, that analysis could plausibly resolve effects the
    current intervals cannot. NOT YET DONE.

  - Training budget follows each backbone's own reference recipe: 200 epochs for
    GPS here, 100 for SAN. Defensible, but a difference along the axis being
    compared, and it must be disclosed when the SAN arm is added.

  - All cells trained on a single GPU model (RTX 2080 Ti), pinned deliberately.
    Wall-clock comparisons across arms are therefore valid; nothing else depends
    on it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. PASCALVOC-SP: A CLEAN NULL -- AND WHY THE BOOTSTRAP CI MUST NOT BE QUOTED
   ALONE HERE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VOC was the strongest available test of whether §3 is a property of positional
encodings or of long-chain molecular graphs, for two reasons. Its graphs are
~480-node superpixel graphs with TIGHTLY CLUSTERED diameters (median 28, p90 30,
p99 34) against peptides' 16-to-159 spread -- so the absolute window (14, 28) and
the relative window (d/diam > 0.5, i.e. d > ~14) nearly COINCIDE, and the two
statistics should agree. And it is the one dataset where a PE makes the task
worse, so it separates "PE present" from "PE useful".

RESULT: a null on both axes.

    arm       rho      95% CI            seed sd   rho_rel   95% CI            F1
    none      0.3276   [0.3235,0.3329]   0.0091    0.3173    [0.3139,0.3214]   0.3929
    lappe     0.3389   [0.3340,0.3452]   0.0067    0.3238    [0.3201,0.3279]   0.3738
    rwse      0.3330   [0.3285,0.3391]   0.0094    0.3188    [0.3148,0.3230]   0.3807
    signnet   0.3265   [0.3214,0.3329]   0.0055    0.3130    [0.3087,0.3177]   0.3595

The two axes DO agree here, as predicted: same ordering, same non-separation. That
is a useful check on §4's explanation -- the peptides disagreement between the two
axes really was diameter heterogeneity, because it vanishes on the dataset whose
diameters are uniform.

THE CI TRAP. One comparison above looks significant and is not:

    lappe vs none on absolute rho: CIs [0.3340,0.3452] and [0.3235,0.3329] are
    DISJOINT -- by 0.0011.

Do not report that as an effect. The per-seed values are

    none   0.3386  0.3267  0.3207
    lappe  0.3304  0.3432  0.3398

whose ranges overlap substantially, and on SEED 0 the ordering reverses (none
above lappe). The bootstrap resamples GRAPHS, so it measures how precisely the
mean sensitivity of these 634 graphs is estimated -- not how reproducible the
result is across training runs. Those are different questions, and here they give
different answers:

    arm       CI half-width    seed sd    ratio
    none         0.0047        0.0091     1.94x
    rwse         0.0053        0.0094     1.77x
    lappe        0.0056        0.0067     1.20x
    signnet      0.0057        0.0055     0.96x

Where the seed sd EXCEEDS the CI half-width, the CI is not the binding
uncertainty and must not be quoted as significance on its own. On VOC it exceeds
it for three arms of four.

Why this does not undermine §3: on peptides-struct the PE effects are 0.043-0.067
against seed sds of 0.007-0.019, so they survive the seed axis by a factor of
2-6. On VOC the effects are 0.001-0.007 against seed sds of 0.006-0.009, i.e.
below the seed noise floor. The peptides result is robust to this concern; the VOC
non-result is what it looks like either way.

RULE FOR THE WRITE-UP: quote rho_seed_std beside every interval, and call an
effect only when it clears BOTH. summary_table.csv already carries both columns.

A note on the SAN arm, relevant to any cross-backbone claim. Its only completed
VOC cells were DEGENERATE -- rho = 0 exactly, because SAN was configured with
full_graph=False (sparse attention over graph edges only) and GT_layers=8, giving
an 8-hop receptive field against a window starting at d=14. Sensitivity beyond 8
hops is identically zero, not small. Those cells cannot support any long-range
claim and must be re-run with full_graph=True or dropped with the reason stated.

9. WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT, IN ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. GPS COMPUTE IS DONE -- 36/36. Nothing further is needed from the cluster for
   this arm unless item 3 is taken up.

2. Add the PAIRED bootstrap. It is the single change most likely to convert
   peptides-func from "nothing separates" into a usable result, and it costs no
   compute -- the per-graph curves are already stored in every result JSON.

3. Rerun signnet at dim_pe 16. Brings it into the parameter band, likely removes
   the batch workaround, and is the only way its sign flip between the two
   peptides datasets becomes reportable. 12 cells, ~3 days at signnet's pace.
   Optional: signnet carries no part of the headline result, and dropping the arm
   with a stated reason is a defensible alternative to re-running it.

4. Decide how peptides-struct's U-shaped curve is presented. It is arguably the
   most interesting single observation here and it is not visible in either rho
   statistic -- both integrate over the window and lose the shape.

5. Report seed sd beside every CI, per §8. This is a presentation change, not an
   analysis one, and it prevents the one wrong claim these numbers invite
   (lappe vs none on VOC absolute rho).

6. Do not draw cross-backbone conclusions until SAN's VOC configuration is
   resolved and its peptides cells exist.
