# Analysis plan and amendments to the proposal's success criteria

**Status as of 2026-07-27: no experimental results exist.** `results/` is empty, every
training entry point in `src/run_experiment.py` is still a stub, and no model has been
trained. Every amendment below is therefore made *blind* — on methodological grounds,
before any number that could be influenced by it has been produced.

That timing is the entire point of this document. Changing a success criterion **after**
seeing results is HARKing (Hypothesizing After Results are Known): it silently inflates
the false-positive rate, because the analyst gets to choose, post hoc, whichever statistic
happened to come out favourably. Changing it **before**, for a stated reason, is an
ordinary pre-registration amendment. The reasoning below is identical in both cases; only
the date makes one defensible and the other not. Do not edit this file once runs begin —
append a new dated section instead.

---

## Amendment 1 — the primary sensitivity statistic

### What the proposal said

> **What success looks like.** Success requires: (a) relative to the No-PE baseline,
> GT+PE variants achieve higher **absolute** sensitivity s̄(d) at large hop-distances d,
> establishing a distance-dependent structural profile that the structure-blind model
> lacks […]

### Why it cannot stand

Raw s̄(d) conflates two things. Model the measured curve as

$$\bar s(d) \approx C \cdot f(d)$$

where `C` is an overall **gain** and `f` is the **decay shape**. The claim we want to make
is about `f`. But `C` absorbs weight initialisation scale, LayerNorm vs BatchNorm
placement, residual scaling, depth, and hidden width — none of which are the positional
encoding. If Graphormer's `C` is an order of magnitude above GraphGPS's, Graphormer shows
"higher absolute sensitivity" at every `d` no matter how fast it decays.

This was tolerable in the original proposal, which fixed a single GraphGPS backbone. The
design has since expanded to three architecturally distinct backbones (GraphGPS, SAN,
Graphormer), which is precisely what makes cross-backbone gain differences unavoidable —
and even within one backbone, PE variants change parameter count and activation
magnitudes, so `C` is not constant across the five arms either.

### Amended criterion (a)

> (a) Relative to the No-PE baseline, GT+PE variants achieve a higher **long-range mass
> fraction**
>
> $$\rho = \frac{\sum_{d \ge d_{\min}} \bar s(d)}{\sum_{d \ge 1} \bar s(d)}$$
>
> evaluated over a fixed, stated window `(d_min, d_max)` identical across all compared
> cells, with a bootstrap confidence interval clustered by graph. Normalized curves
> s̃(d) = s̄(d)/s̄(1) are reported as a secondary view, and raw s̄(d) curves in the appendix.

`ρ` is a ratio of sums, so `C` cancels exactly and algebraically rather than approximately.

### Statistics reported, and their standing

| Statistic | Standing | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| `ρ` | **Primary** | Scale-free; one number per cell; directly rankable; assumes no functional form |
| s̃(d) = s̄(d)/s̄(1) | Secondary | Divides out `C`, but anchored on a single architecture-loaded point — `d=1` is where GraphGPS's GatedGCN branch dominates and where Graphormer has no message-passing branch at all, so it flatters architectures with weak local processing. Its noise also propagates into every other point. |
| raw s̄(d) | Appendix | Reproducibility, and it exposes vanishing/exploding pathologies that ratios hide. On a log-y axis a gain difference is a vertical *shift* and decay shape is *slope*, so the figure is readable even though the numbers are not rankable. |

### Rejected alternative: exponential decay rate λ

Fitting s̄(d) ~ exp(−λd) and ranking by λ was considered and rejected. Graph-transformer
decay curves flatten, because global attention supplies a distance-independent floor.
Fitting an exponential to a decay-then-flat curve produces a λ dominated by the early hops
and a poor fit — the fitted parameter then describes our misspecification rather than the
data. `ρ` is nonparametric: a tail-mass statistic, the ECDF-tail analogue for curves.

## Amendment 2 — uncertainty is clustered by graph, not by pair

Node pairs sampled within one graph are **not independent**: they share a model and a
topology, and their shortest paths overlap. Treating ~50,000 sampled pairs as 50,000
independent observations would understate standard errors substantially.

All confidence intervals on `ρ` are therefore computed by resampling **whole graphs** with
replacement, re-pooling the curve, and recomputing the statistic
(`sensitivity.bootstrap_over_graphs`). The effective sample size is the number of sampled
test graphs (256 per the proposal), not the number of pairs.

Relatedly, curves are pooled across graphs **weighted by each bucket's pair count**. The
number of far pairs a graph contributes scales with its size, and sensitivity itself
correlates with graph size, so an unweighted mean over graphs is biased, not merely noisy.

## Amendment 3 — criterion (b) is underpowered as written

### What the proposal said

> (b) the PE ranking by s̄(d) decay correlates with their AP ranking.

With 5 PE variants, a Spearman correlation on n=5 requires |r| ≈ 0.9 to reach p < 0.05.
A per-backbone, per-dataset test is close to uninformative in isolation.

### Amendment

Per-cell rank correlations are reported as **descriptive**. The confirmatory test pools
across the 9 (backbone × dataset) cells. `scripts/aggregate_results.py` emits both, with
the pooled row labelled `ALL (pooled)`. Criterion (b) is judged on the pooled result.

## Open decision — the ρ window is provisional

`ρ` is a property of the pair (curve, window), not of the curve alone. It is only
comparable across cells when `(d_min, d_max)` are identical, so the window in force must
be reported beside every ρ in the paper.

The values currently in `scripts/aggregate_results.py` are `(d_min, d_max) = (5, 20)` for
all three datasets, and **both numbers are known to be wrong for Peptides**:

- `d_max = 20` against an average diameter of 57 truncates the measurement at roughly the
  first third of the range — a mid-range statistic wearing a long-range label.
- `d_min = 5` is not "long range" in a diameter-57 graph; it captures most of the curve's
  mass.

The intended fix is to define the cutoff **relative to diameter** (e.g. `d ≥ 0.5·diam(G)`)
and index curves by `d / diam(G)`, which also puts the three datasets on a common axis so
a single ρ becomes comparable *across* datasets rather than only within one.

That change is deferred because it is coupled to a **model** decision, not just a
measurement one: GRPE's spatial-bias table is capped at `SPD_CAP = 20`
(`src/pe/compute_pe.py`), so all distances ≥ 20 share a single learned bias. Measuring out
to d = 40 while GRPE cannot distinguish d = 25 from d = 40 would make GRPE's tail
behaviour an artefact of our cap rather than a property of the encoding — and GRPE is one
of the five arms being ranked. Raising the cap changes the model and requires re-training
every GRPE cell.

Until that is resolved, ρ values are **provisional and within-dataset only**. Sensitivity
of the ranking to the window can be checked without re-running anything via
`--d-min / --d-max`, provided `sensitivity_curves_per_graph` is populated in the result
JSON.

## Decisions recorded for reproducibility

- **ρ weighting.** Default weights each *distance* equally, so ρ describes curve shape and
  is sensitive to the tail. `--weight-by-count` weights each *pair* equally, describing
  information flow in a typical graph. Both are defensible and neither confounds the
  comparison — the distance distribution is a property of the dataset, identical across
  PEs — but they give different numbers, so the choice is stated rather than implicit.
  Reported in the `rho_weighting` column of `summary_table.csv`.
- **Jacobian input space.** s̄(d) is computed only over the shared original node-feature
  channels; PE channels are excluded (`n_shared_feats`, enforced by
  `sensitivity.assert_shared_width`). Without this, PEs that add input dimensions score
  higher mechanically and GRPE — which adds none — is structurally disadvantaged. See the
  module header of `src/sensitivity.py`.
- **Norm.** The true Frobenius norm ‖∂h_v/∂x_u‖_F, computed as a sum of squared
  vector-Jacobian products over an orthonormal basis of the output space. Collapsing the
  output first (backpropagating from `h[v].sum()`) measures ‖Jᵀ1‖ instead, which is
  degenerate under LayerNorm and whose error therefore varies by backbone.
