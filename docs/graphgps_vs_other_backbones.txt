================================================================================
GraphGPS COMPARED TO SAN AND GRAPHORMER -- A PLAIN-LANGUAGE WALKTHROUGH
================================================================================
Written 2026-09-26. Every number below was re-derived from results_all/ on the
`results-all` branch and cross-checked against the report draft (graphs (1).pdf,
7 pages, 5 body pages + references + appendix).

Who this is for: anyone who needs to understand what the GraphGPS arm found,
how it sits against the other two backbones, and which parts of that comparison
are solid. No statistics background assumed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WE ACTUALLY MEASURED (two numbers per cell)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A "cell" is one (backbone, PE, dataset) combination, trained with 3 random
seeds. For each cell we record two things.

TASK SCORE -- how good the model is at its job. Each dataset has its own:
    Peptides-func     Average Precision   higher is better
    Peptides-struct   Mean Absolute Error LOWER is better
    PascalVOC-SP      macro-F1            higher is better

rho_rel -- HOW FAR the model's influence reaches. Built like this:

  a. Pick a test graph. Pick a target node v.
  b. Nudge some other node u's input slightly. Measure how much v's output moves.
     (Formally: the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian block d h_v / d x_u. The norm
     collapses a matrix of derivatives into one magnitude, so positive and
     negative effects cannot cancel out.)
  c. Group every (u, v) pair by how many hops apart they are. Average within
     each group. That gives a decay curve s_bar(d).
  d. rho_rel = the share of that curve's total mass sitting BEYOND HALF the
     graph's diameter.

READ rho_rel AGAINST 0.5, NOT AGAINST ZERO:

    rho_rel = 0.5    every distance matters equally (a flat curve)
    rho_rel > 0.5    distant nodes matter MORE than near ones -> far-reaching
    rho_rel < 0.5    influence concentrated nearby            -> "local"

Why a share rather than a raw magnitude: raw sensitivity varies about two-fold
between arms of the SAME backbone (s_bar(1) runs 18.9 to 37.3 across GraphGPS's
four Peptides-func arms) purely from initialisation scale and where the
normalisation layers sit. That has nothing to do with long-range behaviour. A
ratio divides it out exactly.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT GraphGPS FOUND, DATASET BY DATASET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GraphGPS has 4 PE arms, not 5: the GRPE arm was designed but never wired into
training, so it raises rather than running.

PEPTIDES-STRUCT -- the clear result.

    arm        MAE (lower better)   rho_rel    vs No-PE
    No-PE      0.3740                0.5258     --
    LapPE      0.2505  (-33%)        0.4650    -0.061  significant
    RWSE       0.2791  (-25%)        0.4830    -0.043  significant
    SignNet    0.2563  (-31%)        0.4584    -0.067  significant

Every PE improves the task by a lot, and every PE simultaneously moves
sensitivity NEARER. No-PE sits above the 0.5 line (far-reaching); all three PEs
sit below it (local). All three differences are significant on both tests we
apply (see §5).

This is the opposite of the intuitive story. "PEs tell the model about distant
structure, so they should extend its reach" predicts rho_rel going UP. It goes
down, and the task gets much better at the same time.

PEPTIDES-FUNC -- the same graphs, a different task, a much weaker effect.

    arm        AP (higher better)   rho_rel    vs No-PE
    No-PE      0.6285                0.5060     --
    LapPE      0.6537                0.4970    -0.009  not significant
    RWSE       0.6459                0.5032    -0.003  not significant
    SignNet    0.6198                0.5185    +0.013  marginal (see below)

Here a PE buys about +4% AP instead of -33% MAE, and nothing moves much.
SignNet's star is technically earned but should be read as marginal: its
confidence interval clears No-PE's by 0.000062, which is 0.48% of the interval's
own width. A slightly different bootstrap seed could erase it.

PASCALVOC-SP -- PEs HURT the task, and nothing moves.

    arm        macro-F1 (higher better)   rho_rel    vs No-PE
    No-PE      0.3929                      0.3173     --
    LapPE      0.3738                      0.3238    +0.007  not significant
    RWSE       0.3807                      0.3188    +0.001  not significant
    SignNet    0.3595                      0.3130    -0.004  not significant

Every PE makes the model worse. That is not our oddity -- the GraphGPS paper
reports the same thing (0.3846 without LapPE vs 0.3748 with it). And once again,
where the PE doesn't help, the sensitivity profile doesn't move.

THE PATTERN ACROSS ALL THREE:

    PE helps the task a lot   ->  significantly MORE LOCAL
    PE helps a little         ->  no measurable change
    PE hurts                  ->  no measurable change

So positional encodings reshape where sensitivity lives only where they are
actually doing work for the task.

WHY THE TWO PEPTIDES DATASETS DIFFER SO MUCH, despite identical graphs: their
curve shapes are qualitatively different. Peptides-func decays monotonically
(33.1 at d=1 down to 2.2 at d=80). Peptides-struct is U-SHAPED -- it falls to a
minimum near d=13 and then RISES again out to d=80, so the most distant nodes
matter more than mid-range ones. That fits the task: -struct predicts global
geometric descriptors (length, inertia), which depend on where a chain's
extremities are. The PE effect lives in that rising tail: from d=13 to d=80,
No-PE rises +12%, RWSE +59%, LapPE +43%, while SignNet FALLS 17%.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SANITY CHECK: DOES OUR GraphGPS MATCH THE PUBLISHED PAPER?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This matters before any cross-backbone claim. A PE ranking measured on a
badly-reproduced backbone means nothing.

    dataset            published (GPS paper)   ours      difference
    Peptides-func      0.6535 AP               0.6537    +0.0002
    Peptides-struct    0.2500 MAE              0.2505    +0.0005
    PascalVOC-SP       0.3748 F1               0.3740    -0.0008

All three within 0.001. And this is a STRONGER check than it looks: our LapPE
comes from this project's shared cache (symmetrically-normalised Laplacian, 16
non-trivial eigenvectors, zero-padded) while GraphGPS computes its own
differently (unnormalised, 10 eigenvectors, trivial one kept, L2-normed).
Reproducing the published number through a DIFFERENT LapPE definition says the
task metric isn't sensitive to those conventions.

For contrast, the SAN branch does not reproduce as well (§4).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW THE OTHER TWO BACKBONES COMPARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SAN -- same direction as GraphGPS where it can be compared, but far more extreme
and with a reproduction problem.

  Peptides-struct         rho_rel          MAE
    No-PE                 0.2321           0.3962
    LapPE                 0.0202  sig      0.2961
    RWSE                  0.3061           0.3056
    SignNet               0.0237  sig      0.2824
    GRPE                  0.0065  sig      0.2808

  Three of five PEs COLLAPSE to almost zero rho_rel -- ten times lower than
  GraphGPS's shift, not a comparable-size effect. RWSE goes the other way
  (elevated, above No-PE). The same LapPE/GRPE collapse and RWSE elevation
  appear on Peptides-func too, so they are consistent across the two datasets.

  SignNet is NOT consistent: it groups with RWSE on -func (0.183) and with the
  collapsed pair on -struct (0.024), on identical graphs. There is no mechanistic
  explanation available, because in SAN's code all four PEs are routed through
  the SAME dispatch slot, differing only in an internal encoder variant (GRPE
  alone adds an attention-bias term on top). So "how the PE enters the model"
  cannot distinguish them -- a hypothesis the draft originally proposed and has
  correctly since withdrawn.

  Two confounds are partial alternatives, not explanations. On Peptides-func the
  collapse lines up exactly with parameter count (LapPE/GRPE ~928K vs the others
  ~791K). But that does NOT hold on -struct, where LapPE and No-PE are both
  ~928K yet rho_rel is 0.020 vs 0.232. And SAN's probe uses T=128 target nodes
  on graphs averaging 150 nodes, so 54% of its probe graphs are exhaustively
  enumerated rather than sampled -- a different estimator for over half the data.

  PascalVOC-SP is DEGENERATE, not null: rho_rel = 0.0000 exactly for all five
  PEs, with zero seed variance. SAN was configured with sparse edge-only
  attention over 8 layers, giving an 8-hop receptive field against a window
  starting at d=14. Sensitivity beyond 8 hops is identically zero by
  construction. Its task numbers are real; its sensitivity numbers are not.

  Reproduction: our SAN branch is close to published on Peptides-struct
  (0.296 vs 0.268) but far below on Peptides-func (0.479 vs 0.638) and
  PascalVOC-SP (0.174 vs 0.323, about half). SAN ships no official LRGB config,
  so the architecture is this project's own construction. The gap is in task
  performance rather than in rho_rel, so it doesn't overturn the sensitivity
  findings -- but "every PE helps SAN's F1" should be read as a property of our
  branch, not a literature-validated result.

GRAPHORMER -- cannot be compared, and this is the most important single fact in
the whole cross-backbone story.

  Peptides-struct         rho_rel      MAE
    No-PE                 0.5823       0.2638
    LapPE                 0.5798       0.2669
    RWSE                  0.5846       0.2607
    SignNet               0.5772       0.2689
    GRPE                  0.5807       0.2643

  All five numbers sit inside a band 0.0074 wide, and nothing is significant.
  That looks like a clean null. It is not a result at all, because:

      Graphormer's probe used 10 test graphs per seed.
      GraphGPS and SAN used 256.

  That is a 25x smaller sample, and it shows up exactly where you would predict:

      backbone      distinct graphs   interval width
      GraphGPS      692               0.0083
      Graphormer     30               0.0364     4.4x wider
      (1/sqrt(n) from 30 vs 692 predicts 4.8x -- so the width is
       driven by the sample size, not by the model)

  Its interval (0.036) and its seed-to-seed wobble (0.024) are both several
  times LARGER than the entire 0.0074 spread across its five PEs. So the PE
  ordering is being read off differences smaller than the measurement error --
  like ranking four objects that differ by a millimetre with a ruler marked in
  centimetres.

  Consequence: "none of Graphormer's PEs separates from No-PE" is guaranteed by
  the sample size, not learned from the data. Its task-metric spread (3% MAE vs
  GraphGPS's 24%) is equally consistent with a genuinely compressed range or
  with the small sample -- those cannot be distinguished from what we have.

  There is also no published Graphormer baseline on these datasets (Ying et al.
  2021 predates LRGB), so there is no external check either.

  Re-probing at 256 graphs is cheap -- no retraining, just the probe -- and is
  the single highest-value remaining run.

5. WHAT THE BACKBONES AGREE AND DISAGREE ABOUT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Two separate questions, often confused.

QUESTION 1: do the backbones put the PEs in the same order of locality?
Measured by Kendall's W (coefficient of concordance), 0 = no agreement,
1 = perfect. On Peptides-struct, with the 4 PEs all three share:

    all three backbones     W = 0.822   exact p = 0.033   significant
    GraphGPS + SAN only     W = 0.800   exact p = 0.208   NOT significant

All three do rank SignNet/LapPE as more local than RWSE/No-PE. But note what the
second line means: the agreement crosses the significance threshold only WITH
Graphormer included -- and Graphormer is the backbone with 25x less probe data.
Dropping it barely changes W (0.822 -> 0.800) but destroys the p-value, because
a 2-rater exact test has much less power. So the significant agreement depends
on the rater we trust least.

QUESTION 2: does being more local go with a BETTER task score?
Measured by Spearman correlation within each backbone, with MAE sign-flipped so
higher always means better. With that flip, a NEGATIVE correlation is the
"more local => better task" case.

    Peptides-struct    GraphGPS    r = -0.80   exact p = 0.333
                       SAN         r = -0.80   exact p = 0.133
                       Graphormer  r = +1.00   exact p = 0.0167

GraphGPS and SAN agree in sign (more local => better). Graphormer is the
opposite. That apparent reversal is the paper's "sign flip" -- and it is exactly
the number built on the 0.0074 spread above, so it is unresolved rather than
contradictory.

Also note: with only 4-5 PEs per backbone, NONE of the per-backbone correlations
clears p<0.05 except Graphormer's. That is low power from a small number of PEs,
not evidence against the pattern -- but it means the signs are suggestive, not
established.

WHAT DOES NOT TRANSFER, concretely:
  - LapPE is GraphGPS's BEST PE on Peptides-struct (MAE 0.251) and SAN's
    second-WORST there (0.296, beating only No-PE) -- on the identical graphs.
  - GraphGPS and SAN agree that no PE helps sensitivity on PascalVOC-SP, and
    disagree completely on the task: every PE hurts GraphGPS's F1, every PE
    helps SAN's.

A WARNING ABOUT THE POOLED CORRELATIONS: the draft reports a significant pooled
correlation on PascalVOC-SP (r=0.84, exact p=0.00926). Do not read it as a
sensitivity-performance relationship. All five SAN points sit at rho_rel = 0
(the degenerate values above) with F1 uniformly below every GraphGPS point, so
the correlation is measuring "SAN is worse than GraphGPS on both axes" -- a
between-backbone offset. Within GraphGPS alone the same correlation is +0.20.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT IS SOLID AND WHAT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SOLID:
  * GraphGPS's Peptides-struct result. Large effects (0.043-0.067), all three
    PEs, significant on both the interval test and the seed-variance test, and
    confirmed independently by a graph-paired bootstrap. The branch reproduces
    its published task numbers to within 0.001 on all three datasets.
  * The three-dataset pattern (helps a lot -> more local; helps little or hurts
    -> no change). Consistent across three datasets and two backbones.
  * SAN's LapPE/GRPE collapse and RWSE elevation across both Peptides datasets,
    confirmed per-graph by the paired bootstrap.
  * That no single best-PE choice transfers across backbones.

NOT SOLID:
  * Anything resting on Graphormer -- including the headline sign flip and the
    significance of the cross-backbone rank agreement.
  * SAN's PascalVOC-SP sensitivity numbers (degenerate by construction) and
    anything pooled that includes them.
  * SAN's task-performance level on Peptides-func and PascalVOC-SP (branch-
    specific, roughly half the published F1 on VOC).
  * GraphGPS's marginal SignNet star on Peptides-func (0.48% of an interval).

MEASUREMENT CAVEATS WORTH KNOWING:
  * The probe differentiates each backbone's FULL hidden width -- 96 GraphGPS,
    80 SAN/Graphormer, 64 SAN on VOC -- so PE channels are included wherever a
    backbone concatenates them. This is the only definition applicable to all
    three: Graphormer ADDS its projected PE into the same channels, so no
    content/PE column split exists for it and a content-only probe cannot even
    be defined there.
    If PE channels carry disproportionately long-range information -- which is
    what a PE is for -- this inflates rho_rel for PE arms, meaning the
    reductions reported are CONSERVATIVE, not optimistic.
  * The target-node budget T is calibrated per (backbone, dataset) and differs
    widely: GraphGPS 8/32/64, SAN 128/128/300, Graphormer 16. rho is unbiased in
    T, so this affects precision only -- but SAN's T=128 saturates 54% of its
    peptides probe graphs.
  * SignNet is not parameter-matched on GraphGPS: 576,138 against ~503-507K for
    the other three arms (+14%), and its physical batch was halved to 64 with 2
    accumulation steps to fit an 11GB card, so its BatchNorm statistics come
    from 64 graphs where the others use 128.

STILL WORTH FIXING IN THE DRAFT:
  1. THE ABSTRACT OVERSTATES THE AGREEMENT. It reads "GraphGPS and SAN agree ...
     Kendall's W=0.82, p=0.033 on their shared ranking". But W=0.82 / p=0.033 is
     the THREE-backbone value; GraphGPS+SAN alone give W=0.800, p=0.208 -- not
     significant. Section 5.2 states this honestly; the abstract does not, and
     the abstract is what gets read. Either attribute W=0.82 to all three
     backbones, or quote 0.800/0.208 for the pair.
  2. "cutting MAE 24-33%" (Sec. 5.1) -- the actual range is 25-33%
     (RWSE 25.4%, LapPE 33.0%).
  3. Section 4 refers to "Table 1's ~27 diameter figure", but Table 1 prints
     "~20-28". One of the two should change.
  4. "36 of 45 GraphGPS-arm cells" (Sec. 3.1, Sec. 4) counts seeds, while the
     abstract's "37 of 45 cells" does not. Both arithmetics are right; the word
     "cells" means two different things. Suggest "runs" for the seed-inclusive
     counts.
  5. Section 3.2 gives n_shared_feats as "96 for GraphGPS, 80 for SAN/
     Graphormer on Peptides" but omits SAN's 64 on PascalVOC-SP.

OPTIONAL EXPERIMENT THAT WOULD STRENGTHEN Sec. 3.2: re-probe GraphGPS's
Peptides-struct arms at content-only width (96/80/76/64) and compare rho_rel to
the full-width numbers. rho is a ratio within each cell, so differing widths
across arms remain comparable -- which means this is a valid check even though
it cannot be the primary metric (Graphormer cannot produce it). If the gaps hold
or widen, the "conservative" claim becomes measured rather than argued. Needs no
retraining; about an hour of probe time for four arms.
