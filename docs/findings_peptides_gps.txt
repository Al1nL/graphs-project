================================================================================
FINDINGS -- GraphGPS ARM, PEPTIDES-FUNC AND PEPTIDES-STRUCT
================================================================================
Written 2026-09-09, after 24 of 24 peptides cells completed.
Source: results/summary_table.csv, results/*.json, scripts/check_probe_coverage.py.

Scope: gps backbone only. pascalvoc-sp not yet run. The SAN arm is not comparable
yet (its only VOC cells were degenerate for a configuration reason -- see §8).
GRPE is unimplemented on both backbones, so the PE axis here is FOUR arms, not
five.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT WAS RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4 PE arms (none, lappe, rwse, signnet) x 3 seeds x 2 datasets = 24 cells, each
trained to the GraphGPS reference recipe (200 epochs, batch 128, dim 96, 4 GPS
layers) and probed on 256 test graphs.

    peptides-func     T = 8    window d in [26, 80]
    peptides-struct   T = 32   window d in [26, 80]

T was calibrated per dataset; see docs/target_node_calibration_gps.txt for how and
why they differ. check_probe_coverage.py reports 12/12 OK on both datasets: every
in-window bucket populated, and rho identical at count thresholds k>=1, k>=10 and
k>=30, i.e. no dependence on thin buckets at all.

Two statistics, both pre-specified in docs/analysis-plan.md:

    rho       absolute distance, window d in [26, 80]
              PRIMARY WITHIN a dataset
    rho_rel   relative distance, bins 6-10 of 10, i.e. d/diam(G) > 0.5
              PRIMARY ACROSS datasets -- "the axis rho is ranked on"

Their null values matter and are easy to forget. For a perfectly FLAT sensitivity
curve, rho = (80 - 26 + 1)/80 = 0.6875 and rho_rel = 5/10 = 0.5. Numbers must be
read against those, not against zero.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. VALIDATION: THE PIPELINE REPRODUCES THE PUBLISHED RESULT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The lappe arm uses the same PE as the GPS paper's own LRGB runs, so it is the
direct comparison:

                        this work        GPS paper (Table 6, arXiv 2205.12454v3)
    peptides-func       0.6537 AP        0.6535 +/- 0.0041
    peptides-struct     0.2505 MAE       0.2500 +/- 0.0005

Matching to the fourth decimal on both. Together with pascalvoc-sp's earlier
single cell (0.3740 F1 against a published 0.3748 +/- 0.0109), the training path,
the PE cache injection and the metric read-back all behave as intended.

Note this is a stronger check than it looks: our LapPE comes from THIS repo's
shared cache (sym-normalised Laplacian, 16 non-trivial eigenvectors, zero-padded)
rather than from GraphGPS's own computation (unnormalised, 10 eigenvectors,
trivial kept, L2-normed). Reproducing the paper's number through a different
LapPE definition is evidence the metric is not sensitive to those conventions.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HEADLINE RESULT (ranking axis, rho_rel)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
peptides-struct -- ALL THREE PEs SIGNIFICANTLY REDUCE RELATIVE LONG-RANGE MASS,
while improving the task by a wide margin:

    arm       rho_rel   95% CI              vs none    MAE (lower better)
    none      0.5258    [0.5221, 0.5294]     --        0.3740 +/- 0.0263
    rwse      0.4830    [0.4782, 0.4877]    -0.0428    0.2791 +/- 0.0140
    lappe     0.4650    [0.4606, 0.4696]    -0.0608    0.2505 +/- 0.0004
    signnet   0.4584    [0.4545, 0.4620]    -0.0674    0.2563 +/- 0.0020

    All three intervals are disjoint from none's. Graph-clustered bootstrap over
    692 distinct molecules, 768 measurements.

peptides-func -- NOTHING separates. Every arm overlaps none:

    arm       rho_rel   95% CI              vs none    AP (higher better)
    none      0.5060    [0.5004, 0.5120]     --        0.6285 +/- 0.0103
    lappe     0.4970    [0.4907, 0.5034]    -0.0090    0.6537 +/- 0.0102
    rwse      0.5032    [0.4975, 0.5091]    -0.0028    0.6459 +/- 0.0035
    signnet   0.5185    [0.5120, 0.5249]    +0.0125    0.6198 +/- 0.0177

READING. The effect appears exactly where positional encodings actually matter
for the task. On peptides-struct a PE cuts MAE from 0.374 to 0.251 -- a 33%
improvement -- and every PE simultaneously pulls sensitivity NEARER in relative
terms. On peptides-func a PE buys only 0.629 -> 0.654 AP, and the sensitivity
profile does not move measurably.

This is the OPPOSITE of the naive hypothesis that positional encodings extend a
model's effective reach. On this evidence they do the reverse: they let the model
resolve structure locally instead of leaning on diffuse long-range attention, and
the task improves as a result.

Note also where none sits relative to the flat null of 0.5. On peptides-func
every arm is within 0.02 of it -- sensitivity is nearly uniform in relative
distance for all four. On peptides-struct none is clearly ABOVE it (0.526) and
every PE arm below. So the PE effect on struct is a move from "more far mass than
uniform" to "less".


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE ABSOLUTE AXIS, AND WHY IT POINTS THE OTHER WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On absolute rho the picture is different, and for rwse on peptides-struct it is
REVERSED -- both directions statistically clean:

    peptides-struct     rho (abs)    vs none      rho_rel     vs none
    none                0.6266        --          0.5258       --
    lappe               0.6283       +0.0017 ns   0.4650      -0.0608 ***
    rwse                0.6697       +0.0431 ***  0.4830      -0.0428 ***
    signnet             0.5685       -0.0581 ***  0.4584      -0.0674 ***

    (*** = CIs disjoint from none's; ns = overlapping)

    peptides-func       rho (abs)    vs none      rho_rel     vs none
    none                0.5237        --          0.5060       --
    lappe               0.5139       -0.0098 ns   0.4970      -0.0090 ns
    rwse                0.5353       +0.0116 ns   0.5032      -0.0028 ns
    signnet             0.5521       +0.0284 ***  0.5185      +0.0125 ns

THIS IS NOT A CONTRADICTION, and the write-up must say so explicitly or a reader
will assume one table is wrong.

Peptides diameters run from ~16 to 159, median 51, p90 95. The absolute window is
FIXED at d in [26, 80]. For a 30-hop molecule that band is its far tail; for a
150-hop molecule the same band is its MIDDLE. So absolute rho asks "how much mass
lies at 26-80 hops", which is a different question per graph, while rho_rel asks
"how much lies beyond half this graph's diameter", which is the same question for
every graph.

Both are true statements about rwse on peptides-struct: it moves sensitivity INTO
the 26-80 band and OUT of each graph's own far half. Those are compatible because
the band is mid-range for the large-diameter molecules that dominate the far
relative bins.

The analysis plan anticipated precisely this ("the population shift the relative
axis exists to avoid") and pre-specified rho_rel as the ranking axis. The choice
was made before the data existed; it is not a post-hoc selection between two
answers.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PER-ARM SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
rwse    The clearest effect on the absolute axis (+0.012 func, +0.043 struct,
        same direction on both, separated on struct) and a significant NEGATIVE
        effect on the ranking axis for struct. Its seed spread is the tightest of
        any arm on func (sd 0.0018), so its estimates are stable. It has the
        worst task metric of the three PEs on struct (MAE 0.279 vs lappe 0.251).

lappe   Best task metric on BOTH datasets (0.6537 AP, 0.2505 MAE) and the one
        that reproduces the paper. On absolute rho it does essentially nothing
        (-0.010 ns, +0.002 ns). On the ranking axis it produces the second
        largest reduction on struct (-0.061 ***). So the encoding that helps the
        task most is not the one that moves absolute long-range mass at all --
        a dissociation between task accuracy and sensitivity profile.

signnet Separated from none on absolute rho on BOTH datasets, but with OPPOSITE
        signs: +0.028 on func, -0.058 on struct. Three seeds each, non-
        overlapping ranges, so not a small-sample artefact. On the ranking axis
        it is the largest reduction on struct (-0.067) and the only arm above
        none on func (+0.013, ns).
        DO NOT report this as a property of the encoding without addressing two
        confounds -- see §7.

none    The reference. Its absolute rho differs sharply between the two datasets
        (0.524 vs 0.627) even though the graphs are identical, which is a
        reminder that rho measures the TRAINED MODEL, not the dataset.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CURVE SHAPES -- THE TWO TASKS ARE QUALITATIVELY DIFFERENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pooled s_bar(d), the none arm:

    peptides-func     d=1: 33.1   d=2: 9.4   d=13: 3.6   d=26: 3.0   d=53: 2.4   d=80: 2.2
    peptides-struct   d=1: 20.7   d=2: 5.1   d=13: 1.9   d=26: 1.9   d=53: 2.0   d=80: 2.1

peptides-func decays monotonically. peptides-struct is U-SHAPED: it falls to a
minimum near d=13 and then RISES again out to d=80. Sensitivity to the most
distant nodes exceeds sensitivity to mid-range ones.

That is physically sensible. peptides-struct predicts global geometric
descriptors (inertia, length, sphericity), which depend most on where a chain's
extremities are; peptides-func predicts functional labels, which plausibly hinge
on local motifs.

The PE effect on struct lives in that tail slope. From d=13 to d=80:

    none      1.92 -> 2.14   (+12%)
    rwse      1.64 -> 2.61   (+59%)   amplifies the rise
    lappe     0.83 -> 1.18   (+43%)
    signnet   0.75 -> 0.63   (-17%)   suppresses it entirely

Both statistics are ratios, which is necessary rather than cosmetic: the raw gain
s_bar(1) spans 18.9 to 37.3 on func and 9.7 to 20.7 on struct, a 2-4x range
across arms. Any comparison of unnormalised magnitudes would be measuring overall
model gain, not where sensitivity lives.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. CAVEATS THAT MUST TRAVEL WITH THESE NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
signnet carries two confounds, and it is the arm with the most striking result:

    PARAMETERS. 576,138 against ~504,000 for the other three arms -- 14% more.
    It is the only arm outside the reference band (GraphGPS's own LRGB configs
    span 504,362 to 510,453). Driven by dim_pe 32 where the others use 16 or 20.

    BATCH. Physical batch 64 with 2 accumulation steps, where the others use 128
    with 1. This was forced: signnet's phi network runs an 8-layer GIN over each
    of 16 eigenvector channels and OOMs on an 11 GB card at batch 128. The
    EFFECTIVE batch is identical, so the optimisation trajectory is comparable,
    but BatchNorm statistics are computed over 64 graphs rather than 128.

    Neither is fatal, but signnet's sign flip between two datasets built from the
    same molecules cannot be attributed to the encoding until they are addressed.
    The cheapest test is a dim_pe 16 rerun, which would bring parameters into
    band and probably remove the batch problem at the same time.

Other limitations:

  - Three seeds per arm. CIs are graph-clustered bootstraps over 692 molecules,
    so they are dominated by between-graph rather than between-seed variation,
    but the seed axis itself is thin.

  - The CIs quoted are MARGINAL. Every arm probes the same 692 molecules at the
    same seeds, so a PAIRED difference (per-graph rho_A - rho_B, bootstrapped
    over graphs) would cancel the shared graph-level variance and give
    substantially tighter intervals. On peptides-func, where nothing separates
    marginally, that analysis could plausibly resolve effects the current
    intervals cannot. NOT YET DONE.

  - Training budget follows each backbone's own reference recipe: 200 epochs for
    GPS here, 100 for SAN. Defensible, but a difference along the axis being
    compared, and it must be disclosed when the SAN arm is added.

  - All cells trained on a single GPU model (RTX 2080 Ti), pinned deliberately.
    Wall-clock comparisons across arms are therefore valid; nothing else depends
    on it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHAT PASCALVOC-SP WILL TEST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
VOC is the strongest available test of whether §3 is a property of positional
encodings or of long-chain molecular graphs. Its graphs are ~480-node superpixel
graphs with TIGHTLY CLUSTERED diameters -- median 28, p90 30, p99 34 -- against
peptides' 16-to-159 spread.

That matters directly: with diameters that concentrated, the absolute window
(14, 28) and the relative window (d/diam > 0.5, i.e. d > ~14) nearly COINCIDE. So
VOC is the case where the two statistics should agree, and any disagreement there
would indicate something other than diameter heterogeneity.

Prediction implied by §3: if PEs reduce relative long-range mass generally, the
same ordering should appear on VOC. If it does not, the effect is specific to
graphs with long thin structure.

A note on the SAN arm, relevant to any cross-backbone claim. Its only completed
VOC cells were DEGENERATE -- rho = 0 exactly, because SAN was configured with
full_graph=False (sparse attention over graph edges only) and GT_layers=8, giving
an 8-hop receptive field against a window starting at d=14. Sensitivity beyond 8
hops is identically zero, not small. Those cells cannot support any long-range
claim and must be re-run with full_graph=True or dropped with the reason stated.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT, IN ORDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Run the two VOC arrays. Nine non-signnet cells and signnet's three separately,
   the latter at --time=1440 (it trains ~3x slower and probes ~2x slower than the
   other arms).

2. Add the PAIRED bootstrap. It is the single change most likely to convert
   peptides-func from "nothing separates" into a usable result, and it costs no
   compute -- the per-graph curves are already stored in every result JSON.

3. Rerun signnet at dim_pe 16. Brings it into the parameter band, likely removes
   the batch workaround, and is the only way its sign flip becomes reportable.

4. Decide how peptides-struct's U-shaped curve is presented. It is arguably the
   most interesting single observation here and it is not visible in either rho
   statistic -- both integrate over the window and lose the shape.

5. Do not draw cross-backbone conclusions until SAN's VOC configuration is
   resolved and its peptides cells exist.
